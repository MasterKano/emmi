Including operatorship of the Greater Ekofisk Area, plus related infrastructure in Norway and the UK Teesside terminal (a 40.25% stake and operatorship, per ConocoPhillips).
 
The enterprise value is USD 4.425 billion from a 1 January 2026 economic date (the date from which the buyer takes the cash flow). Cash at completion, expected around the end of the first quarter of 2027 subject to Norwegian Ministry of Energy approval, is put at about USD 2.5 billion, net of cash flow earned by the business since the economic date and other adjustments.
 
Including the previously announced BlueNord combination, pro-forma 2026 production rises to about 550 thousand barrels of oil equivalent (boe) a day (kboepd) and reserves and resources to about 2.9 billion boe. The long-term target is now to hold output above 500 kboepd into the mid-2030s, synergies are put at USD 700 million to 1 billion, and fourth-quarter dividend guidance rises to USD 450 million.
 
It is funded from USD 7.5 billion of liquidity, with a gas hedge on most of the added volumes (details below). Eni stays majority owner.
 
The deal puts Ekofisk, the shelf's oldest hub, with a growth-focused independent backed by Eni, and is Vår's third deal since June, after Pandion and the BlueNord combination; the possible equity raise is the near-term overhang for the shares.
The Vår deal in 90 seconds
USD 4.4bn (enterprise value) | USD 10.7 per 2P boe | about 0.7x leverage pro forma (our calc)
Vår becomes operator of Ekofisk, Norway's oldest oil hub, and lifts its long-term output goal to above 500 kboepd, paying about USD 2.5 billion in cash at completion. On a reserve multiple the price is only slightly below Vår's own rating; the open questions are a rising unit cost, an undisclosed decommissioning bill and three deals to absorb since June.
What Vår is buying
• Interests in 13 producing fields on the Norwegian Continental Shelf (NCS), 5 of them operated: the Greater Ekofisk Area (Ekofisk, Eldfisk, Embla, Tor, Tommeliten), plus Troll, Heidrun, Alvheim, Oseberg, Visund, Aasta Hansteen, Grane and Breidablikk.
 
• Output of about 115 kboepd net in the first nine months of 2026, about half gas; ConocoPhillips puts 2025 at about 120 kboepd.
 
• Reserves: 414 million boe (mmboe) of net proved plus probable (2P) reserves plus 101 mmboe of contingent (2C) resources at end-2025. ConocoPhillips reports 221 mmboe of proved reserves. About 75% of the 2P sits in fields where Vår is already a partner.
 
• Infrastructure: 35.05% of the Norpipe oil line and a 40.25% stake in, and operatorship of, the Teesside terminal in the UK.
Price and metrics
• Price bridge: EV USD 4.425 billion from 1 January 2026, less about USD 1.9 billion of cash flow earned since then and other adjustments, gives about USD 2.5 billion of cash at completion; add the business's 2026 tax bill of about USD 1.4 billion, paid in the first half of 2027, and the total is about USD 3.9 billion (ConocoPhillips' figure).
 
• Multiples: on the USD 4.425 billion EV, USD 10.7 per 2P boe (Vår) and about USD 38,500 per flowing boe a day (our calc); the tables use EV throughout. On the USD 3.9 billion total it is USD 9.4 per 2P boe (our calc), and on the completion cash against reserves after this year's output about USD 7 (Vår). The 2C resources and the USD 0.7 billion to 1 billion of synergies come on top.

DNO is about half Kurdistan and BlueNord is Danish; Equinor uses proved (1P) reserves. Pandion is unlisted (its last assets went to Vår, closing in September), so it is left out.
The deal, at USD 10.7 per 2P boe, sits about 5% below Vår (11.3) and about 40% below Aker BP (17.7), and well below Equinor (19.3).
• Per 2P barrel it is in line with Neptune (Jun 2023), Sval (Mar 2025) and Wintershall Dea (Dec 2023), though richer per flowing barrel, and roughly half what Aker BP paid for Lundin (Dec 2021), which bought into Johan Sverdrup's long plateau.
 
• Smaller producers such as OKEA and DNO trade at a fraction of these deal prices, which reflects size, reserve life and, for DNO, Kurdistan risk.
Funding, balance sheet, dividend
• Paid from USD 7.5 billion of available liquidity at 30 September plus cash in the acquired company at closing; its slides say no additional interest-bearing debt is incurred.
 
• Leverage, net interest-bearing debt (NIBD) to EBITDAX (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, amortisation and exploration), was 0.3x at end-September: NIBD USD 3.1 billion against USD 10.3 billion of rolling 12-month EBITDAX (Q3 report). Vår gives no pro-forma figure; adding the USD 2.5 billion completion cash and USD 1.4 billion tax gives about 0.7x on Vår's own EBITDAX (our calc), before the acquired business's earnings (not disclosed) and BlueNord's debt. The ceiling is 1.3x.
 
• Gas hedge: a floor (downside protection, upside kept) of about USD 110 per boe on about 80% of the added gas volumes, for 2027 through the first quarter of 2028, roughly USD 19 per million British thermal units (mmBtu) at 5.8 mmBtu per boe (our conversion). That is well above the USD 12 to 13 gas ABG Sundal Collier says the price implies. No oil hedge was announced.
 
• Equity: Vår will assess raising up to 10% under its board authorisation, subject to markets, and says the deal adds to earnings, cash flow and dividend capacity per share with or without it. A raise would dilute existing holders, including majority owner Eni, unless they take up their share; Vår says Eni will remain the majority shareholder.
 
• Dividend: fourth-quarter 2026 guidance rises to USD 450 million from USD 400 million; the policy of 25% to 30% of cash flow from operations after tax is unchanged.
Why do it
• Scale: about 550 kboepd pro forma for 2026 including BlueNord, and a long-term goal raised to above 500 kboepd into the mid-2030s (about 450 before). Reserves and resources (2P plus 2C) of about 2.9 billion boe cover about 14 years of 2026 output (Vår); 2P alone, about 1.9 billion boe, covers about 9.5 years (our calc).
 
• Ekofisk: the first commercial NCS find, with 4.3 billion boe produced (more than four times the first estimate) out of about 10 billion in place. Vår wants to repeat what it did at Balder and Goliat: extend field life and lift recovery.
 
• Synergies of USD 0.7 billion to 1 billion, net present value (NPV) after tax at 8%: supply chain, transport, gas sales and overheads.
 
• Eni remains the majority owner, which backs the balance sheet and the growth plan.
Risks
• A mature hub: Vår takes on the business's decommissioning liabilities with the shares. It says near-term abandonment costs are limited, but neither company discloses the size of the liability.
 
• Costs: pro-forma unit operating cost of about USD 13 per boe for 2027 to 2029, against the USD 10 to 11 guided in July with the BlueNord combination, a figure that already included BlueNord. The slides don't explain the rise. RBC puts BlueNord's own unit cost at USD 25.7 per boe in 2026 year to date (secondary), a current reading rather than the 2027 to 2029 guide.
 
• Three deals to absorb: Pandion (closed 30 September), BlueNord around the end of 2026 and ConocoPhillips around the end of March 2027, including a large operated hub and its staff.
 
• Prices: the mix stays about 65% oil and 35% gas, and only the added gas is hedged.
 
• Approvals: the Norwegian Ministry of Energy, other regulators, licence partners.
What the brokers say
• ABG Sundal Collier (Sell rating) likes the deal: it puts the price at about USD 9 per 2P boe at end-2026, implying oil at USD 75 to 80 and gas at USD 12 to 13 per mmBtu, 15% to 20% below how the market values Vår on that end-2026 figure (our table, on the headline USD 10.7, is about 5% below Vår's USD 11.3).
 
• Arctic Securities (Hold rating) sees the price at the top of its range, implying long-term oil of about USD 85: full value before synergies and broadly neutral. SEB (Sell rating) is puzzled by Vår's eagerness to buy for cash this high in the cycle. All three flag the possible equity raise.
What it means for the sector
• NCS consolidation keeps going (announcement dates): Aker BP and Lundin (Dec 2021), Vår and Neptune (Jun 2023), Harbour and Wintershall Dea (Dec 2023), DNO and Sval (Mar 2025), Vår and Pandion (Jun 2026) and now ConocoPhillips. Vår's BlueNord combination (Jul 2026) takes the same play to the Danish shelf.
 
• US majors keep leaving: ExxonMobil sold its Norwegian fields to Vår in 2019, and ConocoPhillips now exits after more than five decades, putting the proceeds towards 2027 share buybacks.
 
• Output is concentrating in Equinor, Aker BP and Vår, while smaller listed producers trade far below what buyers pay for barrels.
What to watch
• Around the end of 2026: BlueNord combination completes.
 
• 26 January 2027: Vår's fourth-quarter trading update. 9 February 2027: fourth-quarter results and capital markets update.
 
• No date set: the possible equity raise of up to 10%, market permitting.
 
• First half of 2027: the 2026 tax payment of about USD 1.4 billion. Around end-March 2027: completion, after Ministry of Energy approval.
 
 
 
The Norwegian Offshore Directorate (Sodir) (not listed) published preliminary October production figures.
Sodir · unlisted
 
Average output was 2,030,000 barrels a day of oil, natural gas liquids (NGL) and condensate, up from 1,866,000 in September (an 8.8% month-on-month rise). That was 1.4% above Sodir's October forecast; oil alone was 5.9% above forecast. Liquids were 3.4% below October 2025 (published Friday).
 
Output running ahead of forecast (oil 4.1% above Sodir's forecast so far this year) supports the cash flow of Norwegian producers, including the barrels Vår is buying.
