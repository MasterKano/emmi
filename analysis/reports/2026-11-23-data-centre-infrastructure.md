## Executive summary

1. The build-out has not slowed yet. The four largest United States (US) cloud companies (the hyperscalers) guide to about $730bn of capital spending (capex) in 2026 at the midpoints (our sum), and all say 2027 will be higher. None has given a 2027 number. [1][3][5]
2. It is now debt-funded. Amazon, Alphabet, Meta and Microsoft sold $249.4bn of bonds in 2026 to 4 November, three times 2025. Bonds paid for about 43 cents of every capex dollar over the past year. [12]
3. The bigger pile is off the balance sheet. The Federal Reserve (Fed) counts more than $3trn of signed-but-unstarted leases, take-or-pay contracts and guarantees at seven AI firms, against on-balance-sheet debt heading past $800bn. [14]
4. Rates have turned against it. The US 10-year yield closed at 5.046% on 19 November, and markets price a near-certain Fed hike on 8-9 December. Borrowers at the edge pay 7% to 16%. [26][16][25]
5. Credit is separating the strong from the weak. Oracle's five-year credit default swaps (CDS) went from 37.5 basis points (bp) in June 2025 to 305bp on 5 November 2026; CoreWeave's 2032 bonds yield over 16%. Alphabet and Meta still borrow, but pay double the spread of April. [16][25][13]
6. Our base case is a slower, more expensive build-out, not a bust: capex growth peaks in 2027, weaker developers and neoclouds consolidate, and power and grid owners keep their pricing power. The bear case needs an AI revenue miss on top of higher rates.

:::callout Bottom line
The assets that last are power, grid connections and buildings; the layers that break are short-lived chips financed with floating-rate, high-yield or vehicle debt. In a 5% world, the investor question shifts from who builds the most to who can carry the debt until the revenue arrives.
:::

This report builds on our 31 October piece on debt-funded booms ("History rhymes"). That piece set out the historical pattern; this one tracks what has changed since, with the November credit and rate shock, and what to watch next.

- Amazon kept 2026 cash capex at about $220bn on 29 October and said 2027 will be "meaningfully more". Third-quarter (Q3) capex was $77.3bn, and trailing free cash flow was an outflow of $42.4bn. [3][4]
- Microsoft cut calendar 2026 to about $170bn from $175bn on 28 October, because third-party capacity was delayed, and expects spending to accelerate in the first half of 2027. About three quarters of Q3 capex went on short-lived chips. [2][1]
- Alphabet held $195-205bn and said 2027 will "increase significantly"; it reported negative free cash flow of more than $9bn for Q3. Meta raised its floor to $135-145bn and gives 2027 on its fourth-quarter call. [1][5]
- Oracle gives no dollar figure for fiscal 2027; it says capex peaks in fiscal 2027 and 2028. Its trailing 12-month capex was $75.7bn against $71.8bn of revenue. [19][16]
- Forecasters: Standard and Poor's put 2027 hyperscaler capex above $1.3trn (15 October); Moody's puts the five largest plus CoreWeave near $1trn (27 October). [15][24]

Neoclouds rent graphics processing units (GPUs) to AI labs and hyperscalers. CoreWeave's Q3 net interest expense of $846m was about 2.2 times its adjusted operating income of $379m (our calc), and it guides fourth-quarter interest to $1.05-1.15bn. It says it will issue no more unsecured parent debt through 2027 at current yields, and will lean on contract-backed delayed-draw term loans (DDTLs), customer prepayments of about $9bn in the next three months, and vendor financing. [6][7][8]

Nebius raised 2026 capex but cut revenue to the low end of $3.0-3.4bn because capacity slipped. It expects prepayments and asset-backed loans to fund 75-85% of 2027 capex, and closed a $775m facility at the Secured Overnight Financing Rate (SOFR) plus 2.50% in July. [9][47]

## The big campuses

- Meta Hyperion (Louisiana): about $27bn joint venture, 80% Blue Owl funds, 20% Meta, funded with $27.3bn of bonds. [10][11]
- Oracle and OpenAI (Texas): Abilene "nearly complete"; Shackelford, the next gigawatt (GW) campus, fully permitted; Oracle says the contract is fully funded (20 November). [18]
- SB Energy PORTS-Pike (Ohio): about 4.25GW across nine buildings, leased to OpenAI for 20 years, with Nvidia credit support capped at $105bn. [20][14]
- Brookfield: plans a $27bn AI data centre at a UK power station (reported 6 November). [39]
- Nordics: atNorth sold for $4bn to Canada Pension Plan Investments and Equinix. [37]

## Power is the binding constraint

Chips are no longer the scarcest input; powered buildings are. Nvidia's new guarantees exist precisely because "powered shells are now the bottleneck", in one analyst's words. [20]

- Grid: the PJM Interconnection (PJM), the largest US grid operator, cleared its 2027/2028 capacity auction at the $333.44 per megawatt (MW) per day cap and fell 6,517MW short of its reliability target. About 5,100MW of the 5,250MW load increase was data centres. [32]
- Gas turbines: GE Vernova had 122GW of gas backlog and slot reservations at Q3, effectively booked through 2029. Siemens Energy's gas turbine backlog is 65GW; data centres are about €15bn, under 10% of its €164bn total backlog. [33][34]
- Nuclear: deals are uprates of existing reactors, not new builds. Google and Constellation agreed 890MW of uprates (first in 2028); Amazon signed a 20-year power purchase agreement (PPA) at Calvert Cliffs including a 190MW uprate due 2030-2032. [35][36]
- Politics: on 3 November voters backed restrictions in nearly all of 41 data centre ballot measures across 10 states; Texas's governor-elect pledged a moratorium. Between January 2024 and May 2026, at least 46 projects with more than $170bn of planned investment were blocked or stalled. [39]

## How the off-balance-sheet chain works

:::flow Off-balance-sheet SPV financing
left: Bond investors | PIMCO and others
arrow: $27.3bn bonds
mid: Special purpose vehicle | Beignet, Blue Owl controlled
arrow: builds and owns
right: Data centre campus | Hyperion
up: Hyperscaler tenant | Meta, four-year leases | rent plus residual guarantee
:::

A special purpose vehicle (SPV) borrows against leases from a strong tenant, so the debt sits outside the tenant's accounts. Meta's auditor approved non-consolidation but flagged it as a critical audit matter; Meta discloses maximum exposure of $45.95bn. [11] Nvidia has scaled the idea up: it guarantees the landlord of OpenAI's 4.25GW Ohio campus, with exposure building from fiscal 2029 to a $105bn cap, and OpenAI agreeing to reimburse it. [20][14]

- Oracle (rated BBB- by Standard and Poor's, the lowest investment grade) priced $10bn of notes on 2 November at 240-335bp over Treasuries, coupons 7.00-8.65%. Each 100bp of spread costs it about $1.6bn a year on $155.9bn of debt. [16][17]
- Meta's new seven-year bonds pay 142bp over Treasuries, double the 68bp earlier in 2026; its order book was $58bn against $125bn a year earlier. New-issue concessions ran 50-60bp. [12][13]

- CoreWeave was quoted above 1,000bp in early November, with its 2032 bonds near 77 cents on the dollar. Its contract-backed loans still trade near par. [16][8]
- Banks are pickier: the largest US bank holding companies have close to $1trn of exposure to big tech, developers, AI vehicles, private credit and utilities, per the Fed. [14][25]

## 3. Rates: the squeeze

| Rate | Level | Date |
| --- | --- | --- |
| US 2-year Treasury | 4.770% | 19 Nov 2026 |
| US 10-year Treasury | 5.046% | 19 Nov 2026 |
| US 30-year Treasury | 5.406% | 19 Nov 2026 |
| Fed funds target | 3.75-4.00% | held 28 Oct 2026 |
| Dec Fed hike priced | 94% for 25bp, 6% for 50bp | 19 Nov 2026 |
| European Central Bank (ECB) deposit rate | 2.50% | held 29 Oct 2026 |
| German 10-year Bund | about 3.38-3.40% | mid-Nov 2026 |

Sources [26][27][28]. Under Chair Kevin Warsh the Fed is expected to start hiking, with inflation above its 2% target for more than five years. UBS expects 25bp in December and again in March 2027; futures price more than three hikes. Markets also price ECB tightening. [26][28]

What higher rates do. A data centre is a long-dated asset with a lease-like income stream, so it is valued like a bond plus growth. Higher long yields raise the discount rate on every future rent, and higher spreads raise the cost of the debt that funds about half of the build. The chip layer is worse: GPUs depreciate over a few years, so their debt has to be repaid fast from rental income whose price is falling. Transaction-based rental indices for H100 chips fell 9.2% in the week to 15 November. [45]

- Refinancing wall: at least $1trn of data centre debt matures within five years. Developers face $13.4bn of fixed-rate dollar maturities in 2027; refinancing at October Treasury yields would add at least $380m a year (2.85 percentage points). Neocloud maturities bunch in 2030-2032, about $49bn. [25]
- Moody's flags 2027-2029 as the test for weaker private borrowers. [24]
- Real estate investment trusts (REITs): Equinix and Digital Realty raised 2026 guidance in Q3 on record bookings, and Digital Realty's cash renewal spreads were up 11.8%. Yet on 22 November Digital Realty's dividend yield was 3.00% and Equinix's 2.08%, about 2.0 and 3.0 percentage points below the 10-year Treasury (our calc). Their valuations rely on growth, which makes them rate-sensitive. [29][30][31]
- Hyperscalers can absorb it: Microsoft still expects positive free cash flow in fiscal 2027. The squeeze falls on Oracle, neoclouds, developers and the vehicles. [2]

## 4. Where it is headed

The November shock had four parts: the midterms (Nasdaq fell 5.56% on 5 November), a Fed that is hiking, the resignation of Nvidia's chief executive on 12 November, and a cool reception for Anthropic's $81bn initial public offering (IPO) on 19 November, when Nvidia fell 14%. More than $1trn came off hyperscaler valuations. Credit access stayed open for the strongest names only. [39][26][20][13]

### Base case: slower and dearer, not a bust

- Capex still rises in 2027 but its growth peaks; one broker moved its peak call to 2027 from 2028-2029 after the midterms. [39]
- Big four keep funding from cash flow plus bonds at wider spreads; more equity and hybrid capital, as Oracle did with $10bn of shares. [13][17]
- Neoclouds and developers without investment-grade tenants consolidate; contract-backed loans keep flowing at 7-7.5% for the best. [8]
- Power owners, grid equipment and turbine makers keep pricing power; local politics makes builds slower and dearer rather than stopping them.

### Bull case: revenue catches up

- AI revenue and inference demand grow fast enough that free cash flow turns positive on schedule (Oracle targets the second half of fiscal 2028). [18]
- The Fed stops after one or two hikes and long yields fall back, as UBS argues; spreads narrow, the AI IPO window reopens. [26]
- Vehicle debt trades like parent debt, confirming the leases are wanted.

### Bear case: rates plus a revenue miss

- Hikes continue into 2027 while GPU rental prices fall faster than volumes grow, the telecom 2001 shape.
- A large AI tenant (an AI lab) fails to fund its commitments, triggering guarantees and leaving campuses half-let.
- Oracle loses investment grade, forcing index funds to sell, as with Ford and General Motors in 2005. [15]
- Private credit funds with payment-in-kind (PIK) borrowers and bank lenders take losses; hyperscalers cut capex sharply, hitting suppliers' order books.

:::callout Our view
Base case most likely. The demand signals are still real (Nvidia data centre revenue $107.6bn in its latest quarter, up 110%; Vertiv orders more than doubled), but the financing is now the weak link, and in a 5% world weak links get repriced first. [20][40]
:::

## What history adds this time

- Vendor financing, telecom 2000: Lucent committed $5.7bn of customer credit in 2000; borrower Winstar went bankrupt in April 2001. Nvidia's guarantees are up to $118bn, though most backs one campus with OpenAI as tenant and reimbursing party. [14][20]
- Rate shocks end booms: the fibre boom broke as the Fed tightened into 2000 and funding shut for the weaker carriers first. The 2026 pattern of strong names borrowing while weaker ones are shut out is the same shape (2026 evidence: [13]).
- Shale's borrowing base: banks re-tested shale loans against reserve values twice a year. GPU loans have the same weakness with a faster-ageing asset; watch rental indices as the equivalent of the oil price.
- Railway politics: local resistance to railways in the 1870s US brought rate regulation. The 2026 ballot results are the first sign that data centres face a political cost of their own. [39]
