1 · Norway shuts the data-centre grid queue · Statnett, Cloudberry
Norway shuts the data-centre grid queue: capacity already held becomes the scarce asset
Grid access, not land or power price, is now the binding constraint on Norwegian data-centre growth. Statnett, the Norwegian grid operator, halted new grid capacity reservations for data centres until 1 August 2027 at the latest. Norway has 800 megawatts (MW) of data centres connected, 4,200 MW reserved, 9,100 MW in the queue and requests for a further 18,000 MW, against peak national demand of just over 25,000 MW. The government plans a licensing scheme for existing and new data centres that excludes crypto mining, with its main principles due this autumn.
The queue and new requests together come to about 27,100 MW, more than Norway's entire peak demand (our calc). Sites with connections or approved technical requirements can still proceed, so the pause rewards whoever already holds capacity and freezes everyone else until the licensing rules are written. Cloudberry's third-quarter 2026 presentation says its Selbu project in central Norway has applied for a grid connection, so Selbu waits with the rest of the queue rather than gaining from the pause.
What to watch
Late 2026 (to be confirmed)Norwegian government main principles for the data-centre licensing scheme
1 Aug 2027Latest end date of Statnett's pause on new data-centre grid reservations
Our take. A social-benefit licensing test would favour heat reuse, local jobs and named hyperscaler tenants over speculative capacity. If the rules arrive quickly, the pause is a short reset that rewards incumbents; if they are slow or cap existing sites, growth stalls into 2028 and demand moves to Swedish and Finnish sites.

2 · ShaMaran · ShaMaran Petroleum, Gulf Keystone Petroleum
ShaMaran: earnings arrive, Kurdistan cash starts to follow, politics turns noisier
ShaMaran's earnings are now turning into cash, because the top-up mechanism, which reconciles past Kurdistan export sales to international prices, has started paying. ShaMaran received USD 26.7 million in sales and top-up payments since quarter end, and Gulf Keystone received USD 16.5 million net for its first top-up cargo, over half of the USD 31.3 million owed for September to December 2025. Third-quarter adjusted earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, amortisation and exploration (EBITDAX) were USD 76.0 million, on net output of 14.3 thousand barrels a day, now about 22.2 thousand. Its 25 percent Hamrin stake in Federal Iraq closed on 9 November, and net debt was USD 130.8 million.
Gulf Keystone expects the rest of the top-up by end-November. Back payments plus higher exports should cut net debt quickly if payments stay regular, and Hamrin adds ShaMaran's first production outside the Kurdistan Region before the merger completes in mid-2027. Against that, a drone strike halted the Khor Mor gas field and the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan (PUK) left the Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG); neither touches ShaMaran's fields, but both raise regional risk.
What to watch
End Nov 2026 (to be confirmed)Gulf Keystone's remaining top-up payment for September to December 2025
Mid-2027 (to be confirmed)Completion of the ShaMaran and Gulf Keystone merger
Our take. Regular payments through the winter would make ShaMaran a cash story by the time the merger closes, as the third-quarter results already show. The main threat is a deeper political split, because export payments depend on a functioning regional government.

3 · Genel wins Capricorn as DNO's offer lapses · Genel Energy, Capricorn Energy, DNO
Genel wins Capricorn as DNO's offer lapses
The first contested London-listed oil and gas takeover of this cycle went to the higher cash bidder. DNO confirmed its cash offer for Capricorn Energy lapsed after the shareholder meetings were not held by the 7 November deadline, and Capricorn's board had switched its recommendation to Genel Energy's higher offer of about USD 436 million. Genel's scheme still needs one Egyptian regulatory condition and is expected to complete in the fourth quarter.
Genel gets Egyptian production and cash flow to sit alongside its Kurdistan base, reducing its dependence on a single region at a time when Kurdistan politics are unsettled; Egypt's approval and its payment record on receivables become a central part of Genel's story. DNO keeps its balance sheet intact and is free to pursue other targets in the North Sea or West Africa. Other small London-listed producers with net cash and low valuations now look like plausible targets, and boards have a live example that competing bids get paid for.
What to watch
Fourth quarter 2026 (to be confirmed)Egyptian regulatory approval and completion of Genel's Capricorn scheme
Our take. If Egypt clears the deal promptly, Genel enters 2027 with diversified cash flow. A delay or condition would expose how much of the price rests on Egyptian receivables.

4 · Nordic wind · Vestas Wind Systems, Eolus
Nordic wind: margins mend while orders and pipelines shrink
Nordic wind margins are mending while the forward indicators weaken. Vestas reported a third-quarter margin of earnings before interest and tax (EBIT) before special items of 8.5 percent, up from 7.8 percent, and record earnings per share of EUR 1.14, but firm turbine orders fell 27 percent to 3,377 MW. It narrowed 2026 guidance to revenue of EUR 20.5 to 21.5 billion and a 7.5 to 8.5 percent margin. Eolus swung to a third-quarter operating profit of SEK 112 million, with 450 MW taken into operation or handed over and net cash of SEK 530 million.
Vestas's lower order intake follows extra costs at one offshore project, and the debate moves to whether fourth-quarter orders recover enough to support 2027. Eolus's project portfolio is down to 13,243 MW from 15,820 MW at end-2025 after Sweden rejected its Västvind and Najaderna offshore wind applications, leaving only 147 MW under construction; it has the cash to rebuild but fewer Swedish offshore options, pushing it toward onshore, storage and markets outside Sweden.
What to watch
Early Feb 2027 (to be confirmed)Vestas fourth-quarter orders and 2027 guidance
Our take. A strong year-end order season, after Vestas's third-quarter interim report, would confirm the upcycle and lift visibility for 2027. Another weak quarter would show that higher margins are coming from a shrinking order book.

5 · Lundin Group mining · NGEx Minerals, Faraday Copper, Lundin Mining
Lundin Group mining: a new exploration vehicle and a veteran chair as Chile's copper supply tightens
The Lundin Group is repeating its model: carve early-stage ground into its own vehicle so the parent can focus on a single flagship. NGEx Minerals closed the spin-out of its Valle Ancho project into Valiente Resources, giving shareholders one new NGEx share and one fifth of a Valiente share for each share held, 43.4 million Valiente shares in total. Valiente lists on the Toronto Stock Exchange's venture market (TSX Venture Exchange) as VALX, and the new NGEx shares start trading, on 16 November. Faraday Copper appointed Ashley Heppenstall, former Lundin Petroleum chief executive and a director of Lundin Mining, Lundin Gold and International Petroleum Corporation (IPC), as chair.
NGEx becomes a pure story on Lunahuasi in San Juan, Argentina, and Valiente gives the group a fresh exploration vehicle. Heppenstall brings Lundin Group development experience as Faraday moves from explorer to developer; three rigs are drilling at San Manuel, rising to seven, with resource updates for Copper Creek and San Manuel due in mid-2027. Behind both sits a tight copper market, with official Chilean data showing September output at Escondida, Codelco and Collahuasi well below last year, which also bears on Lundin Mining's Candelaria and Caserones.
What to watch
16 NovValiente Resources and new NGEx shares start trading
Mid-2027 (to be confirmed)Faraday Copper resource updates for Copper Creek and San Manuel
Our take. Valiente's first weeks of trading will show how the market values Lundin Group exploration optionality on its own. A tight Chilean supply picture supports funding for both new vehicles and for Faraday's drilling.

6 · HELLENiQ ENERGY
HELLENiQ ENERGY: record refining margins fund a pivot to renewables
Tight diesel and jet supply is handing European refiners a windfall, and HELLENiQ ENERGY is spending its share on debt reduction and renewables. The Greek refiner lifted third-quarter adjusted earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (EBITDA) to EUR 1,162 million, from EUR 365 million, as its benchmark refining margin averaged USD 30.1 a barrel. Net debt was EUR 1.5 billion and it declared a EUR 0.40 interim dividend, payable in January. It also set out plans to invest more than EUR 1 billion in Romania by 2030, targeting up to 1.3 gigawatts (GW) of solar, wind and battery storage.
The company says Middle East and Russian supply limits caused diesel and jet shortages in its region. That tightness lifts every European refiner with diesel-heavy output, and the third-quarter season shows how much of it is reaching cash flow. One quarter at these margins does more for HELLENiQ's net debt than a normal year, and the January dividend is the first payout from it.
What to watch
Jan 2027Payment of HELLENiQ's EUR 0.40 interim dividend
Our take. If supply limits on diesel and jet persist into winter, fourth-quarter refining earnings stay well above normal and speed up the renewables spending. A reopening of Middle East flows would bring margins back quickly, leaving the Romanian plan to be funded from a smaller cash base.
