• Europe has drifted slightly lower after Monday's rally (live Europe tiles below, about 10:40 Zurich): Paris is the only major market up, while Frankfurt, Zurich and the chip-heavy Euro Stoxx 50 dip, with ASML lower. US futures have turned lower, and oil and precious metals are weaker. The AI relief is fading, not yet reversing.
• Asia caught up overnight: Korea and Taiwan, the purest AI-chip plays, rebounded hardest after Nvidia's board, with US government input, reversed last week's refusal and reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive; he had resigned this month after admitting an affair with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy. Nvidia shares jumped 31.5% on Monday; Hong Kong and mainland China stayed muted.

• Monday's slump in the VIX volatility gauge, edging up again this morning, says fear unwound fast, but the gauge is still well above its mid-November level: investors are keeping hedges.
• The Federal Reserve (Fed) did not join the relief: New York Fed President John Williams said there is still work to do on inflation and did not push back on the December hike markets price. Wednesday's personal consumption expenditures (PCE) inflation, gross domestic product (GDP) revision and jobless claims, in thin pre-Thanksgiving trade, are the risk.
• Today's tests are US consumer confidence and results from Dell Technologies and HP Inc. after the US close. Dell rose 20.5% on Monday, raising the bar for tonight; Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE), a separate company, beat and raised guidance on Monday yet slipped after hours.

3 · Oil and gas
Supply, not demand, is pulling crude lower
Our view. Crude: supply, not growth fear, is the driver. Middle East flows are back and Libya's Sharara pipeline has restarted, so neither the weekend's Gulf shipping attacks nor this morning's Iranian report that three tankers struck mines near Hormuz has lifted prices; the risk premium could return fast. The Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries and allies (OPEC+) meets on Sunday (29 Nov).
Gas: Henry Hub futures fell with the rest of energy on Monday and are recovering part of that this morning; Dutch Title Transfer Facility (TTF) gas trades on European storage and weather, not oil.
Funds: managed money is net short Henry Hub (about 212,000 contracts), so a cold snap could force short covering.
