MORNING PULSE
Huang's return to Nvidia sparks a broad relief rally
Mon 23 Nov 2026 · new since the Sunday look-ahead (Sun 20:26) · Fri 20 Nov close already covered · Zurich time
IN THIS BRIEF  1 Asia & overnight  ·  2 Monitor  ·  3 Oil & gas  ·  4 Top 5 reads  ·  5 News  ·  6 Calendar
• Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive 11 days after he resigned over reports of an affair with a woman suspected of spying for China (AP), and Wall Street opened sharply higher on the news (Reuters). AI stocks, which led last week's selloff, came straight back: Nvidia was up 36% at 16:25 Zurich, chipmakers led, and the S&P 500 is on course for its best day since April 2025 after seven straight losses, with the Nasdaq doing better still. Europe joined in, led by chip-equipment makers. Gold and copper rebounded after last week's steep losses, gold helped by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's warning against overtightening (FXStreet); copper had touched its lowest since March before turning. The volatility index (VIX) has eased but is still well above normal, so this is a relief rally in a nervous market, not an all-clear.

• Bond markets are less convinced. Bessent's Sunday warning that the Federal Reserve (Fed) risks a policy mistake, echoed by hedge fund manager Bill Ackman, removed the tail: traders have priced out a half-point (50 basis point) December hike, which had about a one-in-ten chance a week ago. A quarter-point hike is still priced at about 90% or more on CME FedWatch, and two-year yields are rising as investors sell the Treasuries they bought for safety last week. Today brings the two-year note auction at 19:00 and New York Fed President John Williams at 22:00 Zurich; Wednesday's personal consumption expenditures (PCE) inflation data are the week's real test. The euro is weaker against both the dollar and the franc.
• Crude is lower despite a claimed Houthi strike on a Saudi refinery and attacks on shipping near Hormuz; section 3 explains why.

Our view. Asia's session ended before New York's rebound and was split by stock, not region: Korea took the tail of Friday's AI rout while Taiwan bounced, and the Kospi closed well off a low that was down almost 6%. China's cut in the margin requirement for leveraged stock buying (to 80% from 100%, from 30 Nov, announced Sunday by the Shanghai, Shenzhen and Beijing exchanges) shows Asian policymakers leaning against forced selling. Tokyo was shut for Labour Thanksgiving Day. If New York holds its gains, Asia has catching up to do tomorrow; a short US week with Thanksgiving thins liquidity, which exaggerates moves either way.

3 · Oil and gas
Supply, not attacks, is setting the oil price
Our view. Crude: Middle East exports are recovering and Libya's Sharara pipeline has restarted, so the Houthis' claimed strike on Saudi Arabia's Rabigh refinery and tanker attacks near Hormuz have not lifted prices; Brent touched a two-week low. The Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries and allies (OPEC+) meets this Sunday (29 Nov).
Gas: forecasts of a cold snap lifted Dutch Title Transfer Facility (TTF) gas on ICE Endex. EU storage is 69% full against a five-year average of 87% (GIE, 21 Nov), and South Korea seeks priority access to Qatar's liquefied natural gas (LNG), so Europe competes with Asia for spare cargoes. Henry Hub fell as US weather models turned milder.
Funds: on the managed-money measure, net longs in Brent and WTI are near the bottom of their one-year range, and funds are net short Henry Hub, close to their most bearish in a year.
