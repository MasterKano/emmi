IN THIS BRIEF  1 Equities Monitor  ·  2 Macro Monitor  ·  3 Oil & gas  ·  4 Risk gauge  ·  5 Top 5 reads  ·  6 News  ·  7 Calendar
• The Huang rally is fading. Nvidia fell 5.9% on Tuesday, shedding about $282 billion of market value on renewed doubts that AI data-centre spending will pay back (John Authers), after Monday's 31.5% jump. Asia's chipmakers are hit hardest this morning: TSMC, MediaTek, Samsung and SK hynix lead the declines in Taiwan and Korea, and US index futures are lower again, so Tuesday's chip selling looks set to continue.
• Metals sold off together with a firmer dollar rather than as an AI-demand proxy: gold, silver and copper all fell sharply on Tuesday (copper is the day's largest unusual move) while the euro weakened. Miners fell harder than the metals: First Quantum −10.4% (also hit by a contractor's death at Kansanshi in Zambia), Antofagasta −8.1%, Lundin Mining −5.8% and Anglo American −5.7%. Brent is trading below Tuesday's settlement this morning on supply-glut talk.
• Central banks offer no cover. A December Federal Reserve (Fed) hike is already largely priced, and Australia's October consumer price index (CPI) came in hot at 4.9% year on year, up from 4.3%. The 14:30 Zurich slot today packs third-quarter US GDP, October personal consumption expenditures (PCE) inflation, durable goods and jobless claims into one hour. A hot core PCE is the bigger risk for chips and miners already under pressure.
