IN THIS BRIEF  1 Asia & overnight  ·  2 Monitor  ·  3 Nordic power  ·  4 Top 5 reads  ·  5 News  ·  6 Calendar
• Wall Street followed Asia's Monday lead and sold the AI supply chain hard, with chipmakers and optical-network names hit worst while defensives such as healthcare held up. Asia has now closed lower, Korea still in front and at a six-month low, and China finished a touch softer as a new US rule tightens third-country access to advanced chips. US futures are slightly lower into Europe's open, so the de-risking has not run its course before Nvidia reports after tonight's US close.
• Nvidia's board says it is not considering bringing back Jensen Huang, which leaves tonight's numbers and guidance as the only near-term support. California suing OpenAI adds a regulatory edge to an already nervous AI trade.
• Oil is still softer even after tankers were hit in the Strait of Hormuz overnight, and Vice President Vance said the US does not want a prolonged Iran war, so the market is still trading supply and risk appetite rather than a Gulf shut-in. That reading holds only while exports keep flowing; damage to loading infrastructure would reverse it quickly.
• Nordic prices rose again for Tuesday delivery, with Finland closing much of its gap to the south. In Sweden, Forsmark 3 stays offline until Friday and the cut to SE3–SE4 transfer limits that came with it keeps the south of the country leaning on Germany.

Our view. Europe outperformed the US on Monday because it holds less of the AI hardware chain, and that gap is still the cleanest read on positioning into Nvidia. Korea's close, and the reaction in China to Monday's US chip-access rule, show the shakeout is still about the AI hardware chain rather than a broad risk-off. A strong guide tonight would make this look like a pre-earnings purge; a soft one would test the broader market, not just chips. This morning's UK labour report was mixed — unemployment ticked down but payrolls and vacancies were softer, and regular pay growth cooled a little — and does not take the Bank of England off its wait. US retail sales this afternoon are the other test of whether the economy can carry stocks if tech stalls.
