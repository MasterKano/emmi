Nvidia loses its founder, Treasuries their footing: why stocks rose anyway

Two shocks in one week. On Thursday Jensen Huang, Nvidia's co-founder and only chief executive, resigned from the company and its board after revelations of extramarital affairs, one with a reputed Chinese intelligence operative. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO. Reports say the Justice Department is looking at Nvidia, Megaspeed and Super Micro over possible diversion of export-restricted chips to China. The same week, October US inflation came in hot and the bond market had its worst week since April. Either could have sunk the equity market. It didn't: the S&P rose 2.5% and the Nasdaq 100 3.5%.
Nvidia fell; chips rallied. Nvidia lost 12.7% on the week to 162.85 and hit an eight-month low on Thursday. The rest of the chip complex went the other way: the SOX rose 8.1%, with AMD up about 19% and Broadcom about 18%, the best two stocks in the S&P. Taiwan's market rose 10.0%. CoreWeave beat on revenue and rose 14.5%. Investors sold the man and the governance risk, not the AI build-out — the money moved to Nvidia's rivals and suppliers.
Why the market split it that way. Nothing this week changed the case for AI spending: Applied Materials said chip-equipment demand should stay strong into 2027, and CoreWeave's revenue beat. What changed is Nvidia-specific: a leadership vacuum days before its quarterly results, a Congressional hearing, a possible export-control probe and deals in limbo. Nvidia bonds also slid. That is a discount on one company, and a mild premium for the alternatives.
The bigger shock was in bonds. Headline CPI rose 0.4% in October and core 0.3%, both above forecasts, and core has now run at 0.3% for three months. Fed officials, Waller among them, opened the door to a half-point rise in December. The 2-year jumped 35bp to 4.874% and the 10-year 29bp to 5.153%. Markets now price nearly 90bp of Fed rises by late 2027, about double a week earlier. The curve flattened to +28bp. Friday's PPI (+0.3%) was softer than feared but did not reverse the move.
Why stocks could live with it. Credit stayed calm: US junk spreads fell 22bp to 3.20%. The VIX fell 24.7% to 19.30. Earnings carried the week: the Nasdaq 100 is on track for about 51% third-quarter earnings growth. And last week's post-midterm selloff had already cleared some positioning. The chart above shows it: Nvidia down, the chip index and Nasdaq up, junk spreads easing.
Where it did bite. Rate-sensitive assets that do not have earnings took the hit. Gold fell 4.6% and silver 5.4% as real yields rose and the dollar firmed (+1.1%). Bitcoin fell 5.8%, briefly under $70,000 on Friday. US existing-home sales dropped to their lowest since 2010 as mortgage rates rose. China's tech stocks fell on fears of tougher chip curbs.
What would make it worse, or ease it. Worse: a 10-year held above 5% with the Fed hiking by 50bp; Nvidia's results or a probe that cut its China revenue or its supply deals; junk spreads back toward 4%. Easier: a softer October PCE, a clear permanent CEO at Nvidia, and oil staying near $90 so energy does not add to inflation.

Our view. The market separated a governance shock at one company from the AI cycle, and so far that looks right. The rates shock is the larger risk: stocks are rallying into a Fed that may hike faster. Watch the 10-year and junk spreads more than Nvidia's next CEO.

The week in five points

1. Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia chief executive on Thursday after revelations about his private life, one involving an alleged Chinese intelligence operative; CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO. Nvidia fell 12.7% on the week while the rest of the chip index rose 8.1%.
