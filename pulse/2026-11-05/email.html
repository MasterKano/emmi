• Crude lost most of its war premium in one session. With the vice-president blaming the midterm defeat on the Iran war, the market is pricing a faster route to a deal with Tehran, and it took European gas down with it. Fuel products gave up less than crude, so refiners kept most of their margin.
• Equities read the result as a welcome deadlock. Wall Street and Europe rallied broadly, European banks led, and volatility collapsed. Chips went the other way: voters turned on the data-centre build-out, and Big Tech's campaign money bought little.
