WEEKEND PULSE
Midterms hit AI and oil; the Nasdaq's fourth down week
Week to Fri 6 Nov 2026 · Fri 30 Oct close → Fri 6 Nov close · 12-minute read, then reference lists.

Midterms meet the data-centre revolt: why AI stocks cracked on Thursday

A blue wave with a local twist. Democrats took both chambers of Congress — about 250 House seats and at least 54 in the Senate — and flipped several governorships. Markets first treated it as gridlock (good for stocks): no big tax cut, Fed packing off the table. By Thursday the other reading landed. Ballot measures and campaigns in ten states had targeted new data centres; Democrat wins in Texas, Ohio and Georgia put governors and mayors over sites that hold a large share of planned US AI capacity. Investors who had priced a federal green light for the build-out started to price local vetoes instead.
Thursday's tape. The Nasdaq Composite fell about 5.6%, its worst day in 19 months; the SOX lost roughly 12% on the day and 12.1% on the week. Nvidia −8.8%, AMD and Micron double-digit on the day, CoreWeave −15.4% on the week, and neo-cloud landlords followed. Hyperscalers were mixed: Microsoft and Alphabet held up better than the chip chain; the equal-weight S&P fell only 1.1%. The VIX jumped toward 25.64. It was the sharpest one-day AI drawdown since the March 2020 crash week, on Nasdaq's own note.
Why data centres are political now. Communities have been blocking projects for water, power bills and land use. Between 2024 and mid-2026, researchers counted dozens of AI data-centre projects stalled or withdrawn after local opposition. The midterms nationalised that fight. A Democratic Congress can push for AI testing rules and for utilities to stop shifting data-centre grid costs onto households; statehouses can slow permits. The federal build-out thesis that carried semis and neo-clouds into October now has a state-and-local overlay.
It landed on a soft AI tape. Last week's funding scare (Anthropic's possible junk rating, Alphabet's free-cash-flow miss, Intel's guide) had already sorted winners by who can fund capex. This week added who can still build. Chip Asia joined in: Kospi −11.7%, Taiwan −12.2%. Oil and European gas fell at the same time on US–Iran diplomacy, so the week was not a clean "risk-off" across every asset — it was an AI-and-energy de-risking with a midterm label.
Friday's bounce does not settle it. Nasdaq +1.4% and SOX +4.2% on Friday cut the damage but left the S&P −2.7% and the Nasdaq −4.9% for the week — the Nasdaq's fourth weekly drop in a row. US junk spreads only edged to 3.48% by Thursday: credit still treats this as equity-factor risk, not a funding freeze. Fear and Greed fell to 22.
Two clocks. Near term, lame-duck vetoes still block a federal "AI pause," and hyperscalers can fund a lot of their own power. Longer term, 2028 candidates and statehouses will keep data centres on the ballot. The market is starting to price the second clock into the first.
What would make it worse, or ease it. Worse: binding state moratoriums in Texas or the Southeast; a Senate AI testing bill that hits model releases; junk spreads through 4%; another SOX down-10% day on no news. Easier: clear state frameworks that allow build-out with local benefit-sharing; hyperscaler free cash flow turning sustainably positive; oil staying soft enough that the Fed's inflation worry eases.

Our view. This is a permitting-and-politics shock on top of last week's funding scare. The test is whether data-centre megawatts still get built at the pace the chip cycle needs. Watch state executive orders and utility rate cases as closely as Nvidia's next guide.
